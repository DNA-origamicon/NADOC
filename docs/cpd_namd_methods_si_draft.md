# Preparation and preliminary validation of TT CPD isomers for NAMD

Draft Supporting Information methods. Evidence cutoff: 6 October 2026. The methods below distinguish completed calculations from the remaining cis-anti-I DNA validation. Numerical acceptance limits are project-specific criteria unless explicitly attributed to a published method.

## Chemical scope and molecular identity

We developed a workflow in NADOC for preparing ground-state thymine–thymine cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer (TT-CPD) products for all-atom molecular dynamics with NAMD. Eight ordered product identities were registered: cis-syn-I, cis-syn-II, trans-syn-I, trans-syn-II, cis-anti-I, cis-anti-II, trans-anti-I, and trans-anti-II. Endpoint indices were retained throughout coordinate generation, quantum calculations, force-field assignment, and topology construction. They identify the two original thymidines and do not require the endpoints to belong to the same DNA strand. Numerical parameter sharing was treated separately from chemical identity; registering eight products did not establish eight independently validated force fields.

Each chemical definition specified an atom map, the product bond graph, and four stereochemical sentinels at the two C5 and two C6 centers. Syn products contain the interbase bonds C5(1)–C5(2) and C6(1)–C6(2); anti products contain C5(1)–C6(2) and C6(1)–C5(2). The two original intrabase C5–C6 connections are retained with single-bond product chemistry. Product formation adds no atoms, removes no atoms, and changes neither formal charge nor the pre-existing glycosidic and sugar–phosphate connectivity. In the molecular mechanics representation, the change in hybridization is expressed through product-specific types and bonded terms rather than a bond-order field in the PSF.

Chemical identity was checked using explicitly ordered atom neighborhoods and signed tetrahedral volumes. Signs were compared with the registered reference using the same atom ordering; they were not interpreted as CIP assignments without a separate priority analysis. Coordinate alignments used proper rotations, excluding reflections. Sugar stereochemistry was audited independently of the four lesion centers. This distinction was necessary because an early anti-fragment series preserved lesion chirality while inheriting inverted sugar centers from its starting geometry. Those anti sugar references were invalidated for native-deoxyribose parameter transfer, while their original calculation records were retained.

Structure illustrations for the eight-isomer catalog used registered, stereochemically checked starting cores, including ETKDG/UFF-derived structures. These starting structures were not treated as quantum minima or as evidence of DNA-context compatibility. Detailed additive development and NAMD testing subsequently concentrated on cis-syn-I and cis-anti-I. The remaining ordered identities were not assigned the qualification of these two models.

## Force-field sources and model-compound hierarchy

The additive models retained the CHARMM36 nucleic-acid framework for DNA and used pinned CGenFF reference chemistry where required for lesion development. Source topology and parameter files were recorded by SHA-256, including the February 2026 CHARMM distribution and CGenFF 5.0 dependencies. A project inventory of that distribution did not identify a ready-to-use TT-CPD DNA patch. Generic cyclobutane terms were therefore treated as possible starting analogies, not as a complete lesion force field. The model-compound strategy followed the general CHARMM practice of developing missing chemistry in small compounds and testing transfer after assembly. [CGenFF methodology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2888302/)

Three molecular levels were distinguished. A neutral 36-atom model retained the complete covalently connected lesion core with methyl caps at both N1 atoms. Two neutral 49-atom boundary models each retained one complete deoxyribose, with the opposite N1 methyl-capped and open backbone oxygen hydroxyl-capped. A complete 63-atom d(TpT) model, carrying charge −1, provided a phosphodiester-containing assembly test for an intrastrand lesion. For the interstrand anti product, a separate 62-atom, two-nucleoside fixture contained both sugars and the two anti crosslinks without introducing an artificial phosphodiester connection between the endpoints. Full DNA constructs provided the subsequent context test.

The initial strategy included charged d(TpT) quantum calculations for multiple products, but routine fitting was subsequently organized around the neutral core and sugar-boundary fragments. The charged-boundary protocol used frozen-core density-fitted MP2/6-31+G(d); the neutral fragment optimization protocol used frozen-core density-fitted MP2/6-31G(d). Selected earlier fixed-geometry core energy/response calculations used MP2/cc-pVTZ. These datasets were retained with their distinct electronic-structure specifications rather than combined under a single nominal level of theory.

## Quantum reference calculations and numerical assessment

Quantum calculations were performed with Psi4 1.11. For the final cis-anti constrained-reference campaign, the electronic model was restricted Hartree–Fock followed by frozen-core density-fitted MP2 with the 6-31G(d) basis. Both SCF and MP2 used density fitting. The SCF energy and density convergence settings were 10⁻¹², the response solver convergence setting was 10⁻¹⁰, and the maximum SCF iteration count was 300. Molecular coordinates, atom identities, charge, multiplicity, electronic options, and native energies and gradients were retained with each calculation.

Initial geometry optimizations used OptKing; difficult constrained anti conformations were also examined with geomeTRIC and subsequently Sella. The final Sella protocol used minimum seeking (`order=0`), internal coordinates, `eig=False`, an initial trust radius of 0.1 Å, constraint tolerance 10⁻⁸, and no initial Hessian refinement. The selected glycosidic torsion was constrained while the remaining coordinates relaxed. Independent acceptance required maximum and RMS per-atom tangent-gradient norms of at most 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ and 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ hartree bohr⁻¹, respectively, torsion error at most 0.01°, and preserved chemical identity and stereochemistry. Native optimizer termination alone was insufficient. The projected gradients and constraint residuals were recomputed from saved native coordinates and gradients.

Stationarity and minimum classification were recorded separately. Where full vibrational calculations were performed, the expected internal mode counts were 102 for a 36-atom nonlinear core, 141 for a 49-atom boundary fragment, and 183 for a 63-atom d(TpT) model. Soft modes were examined by finite displacements and step-size comparisons. A constrained stationary point, or positive curvature along a single tested direction, was not classified as a minimum in all unconstrained directions.

For anti glycosidic profiles, competing starting conformations were investigated when matched QM and MM optimizations appeared to select different basins. In particular, an additional endpoint-1 +30° constrained QM structure was found 1.111677 kcal mol⁻¹ below the previous structure at the same constrained torsion. Both structures were retained. Positive directional curvatures at ±0.02 and ±0.04 bohr supported local stability along their separation direction but did not establish complete minimum character. Relative-energy comparisons retained fixed reference identities across QM and MM; separate re-zeroing of individual branches was not used to conceal basin offsets.

## Cis-syn additive model reconstruction and refinement

A published cis-syn parameterization was reconstructed from the Supporting Information of Ma and van der Vaart, DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.7b00215.s001. The source PDF was retained with SHA-256 `f3ae73efc282af07671139946d2dbf58de2172aad8d68085edf300af79681e74`. Atom types and charges were transcribed from Figure S1, and 148 parameter-table rows were extracted with source locations. Forty-three active type/multiplicity groups contained conflicting entries; explicit first-entry and last-entry interpretations were therefore constructed and documented. Because the exact historical dependency files were unavailable and modern pinned parent terms were required, these reconstructions were treated as comparators rather than exact reproductions of the published model.

Native psfgen builds produced a 63-atom d(TpT) fixture, a 634-atom DNA duplex, and a matched ordinary-thymine control. Early structures used deposited 1N4E coordinates. Subsequent DNA simulations used the unbrominated lesion-containing decamer 1T4I, retaining its deposited A/B heavy-atom coordinates and renumbering chain B residues 1–10 as 11–20 for topology compatibility. Hydrogens were rebuilt with psfgen and relaxed for 500 minimization steps with heavy atoms fixed. Every retained heavy-atom coordinate was checked against the deposited source. [RCSB 1T4I](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1T4I)

The refined cis-syn candidate introduced CPD-specific atom-type aliases defined by chemical atom role and original type, shared across the two lesion endpoints. Before fitting, copied numerical parameters were required to reproduce the original energy and forces. The joint refinement varied 109 shared equilibrium bond lengths and angles across the core and both sugar fragments, within ±0.01 Å and ±6° of the registered boundary baseline. Force constants, charges, proper torsions, Lennard–Jones parameters, and Urey–Bradley terms remained fixed in this refinement. Derivatives were obtained from the stationary-minimum Hessian response, whereas acceptance used explicitly re-minimized geometries and independently recomputed checks.

The final v6 overlay retained only terms involving a CPD-specific type. This restriction prevented unintended alteration of ordinary DNA, for which the overlay produced exactly zero energy and force change in the control test. Export checks restored explicit Urey–Bradley terms and sufficient equilibrium-value precision where the parameter writer omitted or rounded them, propagated applicable CUFIX NBFIX terms to aliases, and verified native PSF title records. Two reactant planar C5 impropers were removed; the tested carbonyl impropers were retained.

Across the three training fragments, all 142 bond and 270 angle comparisons met the respective 0.03 Å and 3° limits. Maximum bond errors were 0.027451, 0.028815, and 0.023046 Å for the core and endpoints 1 and 2; corresponding maximum angle errors were 2.568227°, 2.882661°, and 2.885473°. All 18 monitored stereocenters retained their reference signs. Maximum residual forces were below 6.1 × 10⁻⁶ kcal mol⁻¹ Å⁻¹, and step-halved internal-curvature checks were positive. These optimized geometries were training evidence. An additional fixed-geometry core-energy evaluation gave RMSE 0.563453 kcal mol⁻¹ and maximum error 0.830392 kcal mol⁻¹ over six low-energy nonreference structures. This did not test the complete glycosidic free-energy landscape in solution.

## Cis-anti additive electrostatics and bonded refinement

Corrected cis-anti-I core and sugar references were developed as a separate ordered-endpoint model. Endpoint-specific types were introduced where shared types forced geometrically distinct environments to use the same parameters. The additive potential retained harmonic bond and angle terms, Urey–Bradley terms, CHARMM proper Fourier terms, impropers, Coulomb interactions, and Lennard–Jones interactions. Proper terms used the convention K[1 + cos(nφ − δ)], including the constant contribution during energy/export comparisons.

The final electrostatic development dataset contained 17 clean water-interaction curves and three HF dipole vectors spanning the retained conformers. An additional mixed-site O4 curve was excluded on the basis of its contact-specificity audit; it was not silently treated as a clean single-site target. Water calculations used the registered HF/6-31G(d) additive-CHARMM protocol, including the 1.16 interaction-energy scale and no counterpoise correction. Probes targeted O2, O4, and H3 at both ordered endpoints. The original screened grids spanned 1.5–2.7 Å in 0.2 Å increments, selecting among water azimuths 0°, 120°, and 240° by non-target clearance (at least 1.1 times summed covalent radii). QM minima were estimated by local quadratic interpolation; the polar distance target was shortened by 0.2 Å. MM minima were evaluated on a 1.1–5.0 Å grid with 0.02 Å spacing, including CHARMM TIP3P hydrogen Lennard–Jones interactions. Sugar, cap, and aliphatic-hydrogen charges and Lennard–Jones terms were fixed. Remaining ordered base charges could vary by ±0.15e subject to exact total-charge conservation.

The charge objective used actual MM interaction-energy minima, their distances, and dipole-vector components. Residual scales were 0.2 kcal mol⁻¹ for interaction energies, 0.1 Å for distances, and 0.5 D for dipole components, with each block normalized by the square root of its size. Charge regularization contributed residuals 0.2 Δq/(0.1e √Nq). ESP errors were reported diagnostically rather than fitted in this stage. All 17 development curves met the fixed maximum-error criteria of 0.5 kcal mol⁻¹ and 0.2 Å; independently reconstructed maxima were 0.41045 kcal mol⁻¹ and 0.12391 Å. Dipole-vector errors were 0.441, 0.878, and 0.986 D. These are exposed development results, not independent water-transfer validation.

Conformational refinement initially used 19 retained QM profile/reference records. Four subsequently evaluated ±22.5° targets were classified as exposed after their results informed further development. The final development set retained these 23 records and added the independently checked lower endpoint-1 +30° branch, giving 24 energy/shape targets plus three representative geometries. The original 23-point energy RMSE was checked separately to prevent the added target from diluting an existing failure.

The final adjustable set contained 22 proper-torsion coefficients: 12 attachment coefficients and ten coefficients from six ordered ring families. For each endpoint e, the additional families were C2(e)–N1(e)–C6(e)–C5(other), with multiplicities 3 and 6; N3(e)–C2(e)–N1(e)–C6(e), with multiplicities 2 and 4; and N1(e)–C2(e)–N3(e)–C4(e), with multiplicity 2. Coefficient changes remained within ±5 kcal mol⁻¹ of the same registered baseline, with phases fixed at 0° or 180°. Charges, Lennard–Jones terms, bond and angle parameters, impropers, unselected proper terms, and parent DNA parameters were fixed during this conformational stage.

For each parameter vector, MM structures were optimized from fixed QM starting coordinates with Sella and assessed independently. Constrained structures retained their measured QM scan angle. Numerical acceptance required preserved chemistry and maximum projected force below 0.001 kcal mol⁻¹ Å⁻¹; unconstrained cases used the full force. Shape criteria were aligned heavy-atom RMSD ≤0.25 Å and maximum mapped heavy-atom proper-torsion discrepancy ≤20°. Representative lesion/attachment geometries additionally required maximum bond and angle errors ≤0.03 Å and ≤3°. Relative energies required RMSE ≤1 and maximum absolute error ≤2 kcal mol⁻¹.

The final v3 fitting implementation computed fresh finite-difference sensitivities for all 22 coefficients and used SLSQP to solve a bounded minimax subproblem on linearized, normalized acceptance measures. Proposed trust-region steps were evaluated through actual molecular reoptimization. This implementation differs from the earlier v2 trust-region reflective least-squares optimizer. The residual record also retained scaled energy, aligned-coordinate, proper-torsion, representative-bond, and representative-angle blocks, coefficient regularization of 0.05 times the mean squared shift normalized by 3 kcal mol⁻¹, and a residual of 1000 for a numerically failed fragment. Failed structures remained in the record.

The final round stopped at the first complete development pass, model 48, after 48 evaluated parameter vectors. Development energy RMSE and maximum error were 0.471083 and 1.287714 kcal mol⁻¹; worst heavy-atom RMSD and proper-torsion discrepancy were 0.240585 Å and 19.2133°. The frozen parameter file had SHA-256 `b5d295e934910ef5ef0b3ea960f12155875d4943476ceb33d1974f2eba49b547`.

After freezing that candidate, four new constrained QM targets were acquired at ±18.75° about the endpoint-1 and lower endpoint-2 references. Their absolute torsions were −93.8659559°, −56.3659559°, 65.7159437°, and 103.2159437°. An exposure audit found no matches within 0.01° among 905 explicit historical angle records. Each target received one QM-seeded MM scoring attempt using the locked candidate and the existing common energy references. All four passed the registered numerical, chemical, energy, and shape criteria; energy RMSE was 0.425150 kcal mol⁻¹ and maximum absolute error was 0.723403 kcal mol⁻¹. These tests establish local prospective conformational performance within the sampled regions, not complete torsional-basin coverage or solution populations.

## Full topology assembly and implementation checks

Product topology was generated with native psfgen and checked against the reactant atom inventory and registered graph delta. Audits verified unique atom identities, total charge, masses, exact crosslinks, retained intrabase and backbone bonds, regenerated angle/dihedral coverage, and the intended improper changes. No missing term was replaced silently with a generic parameter. Matching typed PSFs and parameter overlays were used together; mass-only alias topology files were not treated as complete lesion patches. High-precision fitted charges were restored where six-decimal PSF serialization introduced a small net-charge residual, with the raw psfgen output retained.

The cis-anti DNA context was the frozen `2hb_1xT_CPD` construct, containing 96 nucleotides in three strands and 3,043 solute atoms. The ordered lesion endpoints mapped to D001:15 and D000:8 in the generated topology. The patch reassigned 28 base atoms, added the two anti crosslinks, removed the two reactant planar C5 impropers, and retained native DNA sugar/phosphate particle types and charges. The solute charge remained −93e and the lesion pair charge remained −2e, although individual lesion residue charges were fractional. Pair-charge conservation, rather than forcing each residue separately to −1e, was used in the audit.

The initial DNA transfer audit verified 222 exact lesion/attachment coefficient transfers, all 28 lesion mass/Lennard–Jones assignments, and invariance of 3,015 unaffected particles and unaffected parent terms. Local lesion/attachment corrections were confined to their intended typed scope. Transfer of the final conformational candidate changed precisely ten intended proper-torsion occurrences in anti DNA and zero in the undamaged control; masses and non-proper forces were unchanged at that update.

OpenMM Reference and native NAMD energies and forces were compared at identical coordinates before dynamics. Tests included fragment starting/minimum structures, the full solute, and a small solvent/ion fixture. Unswitched, effectively no-cutoff comparisons isolated implementation consistency without claiming independent agreement of the complete periodic PME calculation. The cis-syn boundary comparisons reached maximum energy and force discrepancies of 0.000401 kcal mol⁻¹ and 0.000441 kcal mol⁻¹ Å⁻¹. The final anti full-system workflow additionally compared native CPU-bonded reference and GPU-resident calculations on the exact solvated composition. For those comparisons the permitted discrepancies were max(0.001 kcal mol⁻¹, 10⁻⁴|Eref|) for energy and max(0.001 kcal mol⁻¹ Å⁻¹, 10⁻⁴ max|Fref|) for the maximum force-component error. Relative allowances were reported explicitly where used.

## Cis-anti DNA coordinate construction

Rigid placement alone did not provide a usable interstrand seed. The source C1′ separation was 4.07694 Å, compared with 7.12611 Å in the tested anti fragment. A four-anchor base fit compressed the two glycosidic bonds to approximately 1 Å, while a whole-nucleoside fit introduced contacts and a phosphate-bond/ring piercing. Those rejected structures were retained separately and were not passed directly to dynamics.

An independent sugar audit identified defective C3′ configurations at D000:7 and D002:26. Canonical neighbor orderings were C1′: O4′, C2′, glycosidic N, H1′; C3′: C2′, C4′, O3′, H3′; and C4′: C3′, C5′, O4′, H4′. Under the implemented four-neighbor volume convention, the required signs were negative, positive, and positive, respectively, using N1 for pyrimidines and N9 for purines. O3′ and H3′ at the two defective sites were reconstructed while C3′, C2′, and C4′ remained fixed. O3′ placement reproduced the two parent 109.7° equilibrium angles; the native sugar sign selected the mirror solution. C3′–O3′ and C3′–H3′ lengths were 1.433 and 1.111 Å.

The reconstructed structures underwent staged native minimization: 500 steps with solute fixed; 1,500 steps with the two repaired sugar frames protected, heavy positional restraint strength 5 kcal mol⁻¹ Å⁻², and temporary sugar improper strength 50 kcal mol⁻¹ rad⁻²; 1,500 steps with no fixed atoms, positional strength 1 and sugar improper strength 50; 1,500 steps with only sugar improper strength 10; and 5,000 steps with all artificial restraints removed. Water remained rigid. Temporary restraints covered the 288 sugar centers and were checked by independent extra-restraint energy/force differences. They were construction controls, not fitted lesion parameters.

All saved construction frames retained 298 anti or 288 control monitored stereochemical signs. Final structures had no severe solute contacts or ring piercings and repaired the adjoining O3′–P distances to approximately 1.57–1.59 Å. Nevertheless, the full-system stationarity and original product-displacement screens failed: maximum solute forces were approximately 36–39 kcal mol⁻¹ Å⁻¹, and maximum anti base displacement was 8.25 Å. These structures were therefore treated as chemically intact engineering seeds, not certified minima or successful rigid product placements. Subsequent isolated 100 ps NAMD tests established short-time engine integrity. Historical anti/control conditioning differed and remains a limitation of causal comparisons between the conditions.

## Solvation and periodic-cell correction

The final cis-anti/control preparations used matched explicit CHARMM TIP3P solvent and NaCl. An empty GROMACS water packing was converted to TIP3P geometry, with O–H length 0.9572 Å and H–O–H angle 104.52°. Water selection used the union of the anti and control solute coordinates so that both systems received exactly the same solvent and ion positions. Retained waters required oxygen–solute separation ≥3 Å and every water-atom–solute separation ≥1.5 Å. Waters were replaced by ions using a deterministic random permutation, with ion–solute and ion–ion minimum separations of 5 Å and ion-placement seed 41017.

Earlier fixed-volume and elongated-cell tests developed excessive tension/cavities or insufficient solute–image separation. An elongated-box anti validation reached a saved-frame image distance of 11.7615 Å, below the unchanged 12 Å criterion, despite native completion of its segment. That trajectory was retained as failed isolation evidence. The replacement cell was a 140 Å cube, prepared from the original pre-startup solute seeds rather than failed trajectory coordinates. The largest previously observed solute diameter was 112.571640 Å. For the recorded conformations, a shortest lattice vector greater than this diameter plus 12 Å provides a rotation-independent clearance bound; subsequent conformational expansion and NPT contraction still required direct monitoring.

Salt-pair count was calibrated using the pooled late-500-ps volume of an anti/control cubic NPT diagnostic, 2,625,874.08862 Å³. The count was selected prospectively as the nearest integer to 0.150 V × 6.02214076 × 10⁻⁴, for V in Å³. This gave 237 added NaCl pairs. An additional 93 sodium ions neutralized the DNA. Salt concentration refers to added pairs divided by total periodic-cell volume, excluding neutralizing sodium.

Each final system contained 265,477 atoms: 3,043 solute atoms, 87,289 waters, 330 sodium ions, and 237 chloride ions, with total mass 1,617,952.3015 Da. Solute identities, graph, ordinary masses, parameter coverage, neutrality, identical solvent/ion coordinates, and unchanged force-field bytes were verified. The final preparations yielded late-window added-salt concentrations of 149.842–149.867 mM; the diagnostic composition was not itself reused as the final concentration claim.

## Final cis-anti NAMD preparation protocol

Native simulations used the pinned local NAMD Git-2025-12-04 build with GPU-resident execution on an NVIDIA RTX 3080 Ti and four CPU workers. Ordinary atomic masses and a 2 fs timestep were used, without hydrogen-mass repartitioning. Bonds involving hydrogen were constrained (`rigidBonds all`), SETTLE was enabled for water, the rigid tolerance was 10⁻⁸, and the maximum rigid iteration count was 200. Short-range nonbonded and full electrostatic forces were evaluated every step; the neighbor-list cycle was ten steps.

Van der Waals force switching operated from 10 to 12 Å, with a 14 Å pair-list distance and 2 Å margin. The CHARMM exclusion convention was `exclude scaled1-4` with `oneFourScaling 1`. PME used tolerance 10⁻⁶ and a 144 × 144 × 144 grid. The force-switch choice was explicitly requalified after earlier switching/ensemble diagnostics; it was not assumed equivalent to the previous Hamiltonian. [NAMD nonbonded conventions](https://www.ks.uiuc.edu/Research/namd/3.0.2/ug/node25.html)

Temperature control used Langevin dynamics with damping 1 ps⁻¹ and hydrogen thermostatting disabled. NPT used an isotropic Langevin piston at 1.01325 bar and 300 K, with period 200 fs and decay 100 fs, group pressure enabled, flexible-cell deformation disabled, and constant-area mode disabled. Binary coordinates, velocities, and the complete XSC extended-system state were retained for restarts.

One common 1,000-step minimization was performed for each chemistry. Three anti/control replica pairs used seeds 41017, 52027, and 63037. Each replica began from its chemistry's common minimized coordinates, with velocities initialized at 50 K. Ten successive 5 ps fixed-volume heating stages set thermostat targets of 75, 100, …, 300 K, giving 50 ps heating. This was followed by 1 ns NPT at 300 K and a 10 ps NPT restart test. Stage-specific random seeds were derived from the paired seed. Continuation preserved coordinates, velocities, and cell state but did not imply bitwise preservation of the stochastic random-number stream.

Heating coordinates were saved every 1 ps; NPT and restart-preparation coordinates and cell records were saved every 2 ps. Complete coordinate/velocity/XSC restart sets were retained every 100 ps, with explicit stage endpoints. Analyses checked saved chemistry, water geometry and contacts, actual periodic cells, >12 Å solute–image clearance over the 26 neighboring images, finite native energies, endpoint identity, and restart-potential continuity. Quantization-aware water checks distinguished trajectory storage precision from constraint failure. Independent audits replayed selected frames and binary endpoints and checked all saved cell records; those audits were not described as independent all-frame chemical reanalysis.

All six final preparations completed these checks. Across them, the minimum saved NPT image separation was 31.417–40.110 Å and late-window density was 1.022940–1.023110 g cm⁻³. Transient heating voids diminished during NPT. Density, temperature, pressure blocks, and spatial void witnesses were reviewed as physical diagnostics; a short stable history was not treated as proof of equilibrium.

## DNA validation completed to date and remaining scope

For cis-syn-I, three lesion duplexes and three matched undamaged controls were initially prepared with CHARMM TIP3P, sodium counterions and nominal 150 mM added NaCl. The short pilot used native GPU NAMD, PME, ordinary masses, 2 fs integration, rigid hydrogen bonds, 300 K, and 1.01325 bar. Following 10,000 minimization steps, 100 ps staged NPT equilibration applied restraint scales 1, 0.5, and 0.1 for 20 ps each and zero for the final 40 ps, followed by 100 ps unrestrained NPT. The early small-box campaign was subsequently found to suffer periodic-image contamination and was not used as evidence of isolated solution behavior.

The revised cis-syn campaign used deposited 1T4I starting coordinates and 90 Å cubic boxes. All six revised short pilots passed their recorded checks. A subsequent bounded continuation accumulated 34 ns over 68 half-nanosecond blocks and 17,000 audited frames. Lesion/control replica 1 each reached 6 ns, and the other replicas reached 5.5 ns; comparative summaries used the common 5.5 ns interval. The conservative image-clearance lower bound remained above 43.60 Å. One lesion replica developed persistent opening of the lesion-opposing A6–B15 pair while retaining intact lesion bonds and stereochemistry. Accordingly, this benchmark supports finite-duration structural and numerical stability, not converged opening populations. Controls inherited lesion-crystal starting geometry, another limitation of equilibrium comparisons. Subsequent general-builder integration was verified separately in a 12-bp, 40,061-atom system with 1,000 minimization and 1,000 dynamics steps at 2 fs.

For cis-anti-I, the registered next validation stage comprises three lesion and three control trajectories of 10 ns each, advanced in sequential 1 ns NPT segments. At the evidence cutoff, all six final cubic preparations were audited and the first anti validation run had been admitted; the six-run validation endpoint had not been completed. Validation uses 10 ps coordinate output, 2 ps energy output, 100 ps complete restart sets, and explicit 1 ns endpoints. Failed chemistry, image isolation, native execution, or restart checks stop further admission. Earlier failed or provisional trajectories do not count toward the six final trajectories.

Registered structural observables include lesion and attachment distances, glycosidic and sugar torsions, local polar-atom contacts, and base-ring opening/stacking descriptors. Comparisons retain common atom-pair identities because the condition-specific initial contact lists contain different numbers of pairs (35 anti and 34 control). Contact geometry alone is not labeled a hydrogen bond. The planned review includes each replica and first/last 5 ns summaries rather than treating global RMSD stabilization or elapsed duration as an equilibrium certificate.

## Exploratory polarizable branch

A separate cis-anti Drude investigation examined permanent charges, polarizabilities, Thole factors, carbonyl anisotropy, lone pairs, and SWM4-NDP water interactions. A graph audit found that an early MM evaluator and NAMD topology used syn crosslinks on an anti QM geometry. Earlier statistical and cross-engine passes therefore did not qualify the intended anti product. Together with the independent sugar-identity findings, this required preserving but withholding those results from native-sugar/full-product validation. The polarizable work is an exploratory branch; it is not part of the additive NAMD qualification described above and does not establish a released anti Drude model.

## Reproducibility and qualification boundaries

Source structures, atom maps, electronic-structure inputs, native outputs, parameter files, policies, optimization histories, and simulation checkpoints were associated with immutable file hashes. Development and prospective datasets were distinguished explicitly, and targets consumed during fitting were not subsequently called independent validation. Failed calculations and superseded methods remained identifiable in their original evidence directories. Native software completion, correct implementation, chemical coordinate integrity, local QM agreement, short-time dynamics, and DNA-context qualification were treated as separate outcomes.

The present workflow supports preliminary additive cis-syn-I simulations for the tested adjacent internal intrastrand context and a fitted, prospectively tested cis-anti-I candidate with completed matched solvated preparations. It does not yet establish complete cis-anti DNA structural qualification, equilibrium lesion populations, quantitative mechanics, photochemical reaction rates, or validation of all eight ordered isomers. Neither the visualization catalog nor a successful short NAMD trajectory substitutes for these remaining tests.
