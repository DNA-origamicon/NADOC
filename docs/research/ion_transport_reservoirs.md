# Reservoir treatments for ion transport simulations

Research date: 2026-09-05. Scope: published methods and their applicability to NADOC's DNA-on-graphene, explicit-solvent NAMD workflow.

## Findings

Published studies use both fully explicit reservoirs and reduced reservoir models. “Implicit solvent,” “implicit external reservoir,” and “maintained concentration” mean different things. A concentration controller can operate on fully explicit water and ions; it does not by itself reduce the solvent atom count.

| Approach and example | What is represented explicitly? | How reservoirs and driving are implemented | Implication for NADOC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applied-field atomistic MD: Aksimentiev & Schulten (2005), α-hemolysin; Sahu & Zwolak (2018), graphene | Water, ions, channel/membrane sites within a periodic box | Solvent compartments communicate through a pore and periodic boundaries. An external electric field drives current. For a uniform field, the applied periodic-cell voltage is E times the cell length along the field. Reservoir dimensions affect access resistance. | Closest to the existing NAMD approach. Keep hydration and DNA flexibility, but demonstrate size convergence and calibrate bulk conductivity. |
| Computational electrophysiology (CompEL): Kutzner et al. (2011) | Explicit water, ions and two membrane/channel copies | Two membranes create two aqueous compartments under PBC. A controlled charge imbalance generates voltage. Ion/water exchanges in bulk buffer regions maintain concentration and/or charge imbalance despite permeation. | Maintains electrochemical gradients, but still pays for explicit reservoirs and a double-membrane system. A different workflow, not an implicit-water switch. |
| Grand-canonical Monte Carlo / Brownian dynamics: Im, Seefeld & Roux (2000) | Individual mobile ions in the inner and buffer regions; microscopic channel geometry enters the interaction model | Water is a continuum dielectric; ion motion is overdamped stochastic dynamics. Ion insertion/deletion in buffer regions uses reservoir chemical potentials. Outer bulk ions are represented implicitly. Electrostatic boundary conditions impose the transmembrane potential. | A well-established way to avoid explicit bulk water. Requires a separate transport model with calibrated diffusion coefficients and electrostatic/short-range interaction maps. |
| Nernst–Planck + local-equilibrium Monte Carlo (NP+LEMC): Boda & Gillespie (2012) | Sampled particles within a reduced electrolyte model; no atomistic water trajectories | Divide space into volume elements with local electrochemical potentials. Grand-canonical sampling obtains concentrations; iterate Nernst–Planck fluxes and continuity to a steady state. Boundary electrochemical potentials supply the reservoir conditions. | Useful for reduced transport calculations and correlations. It does not directly preserve atomistic hydration kinetics or an MD trajectory of flexible DNA. |
| Constant chemical potential MD (CμMD): Perego, Salvalaglio & Parrinello (2015) | Explicit molecules in the process, control and reservoir regions | A spatially localized feedback force regulates molecular transport between a control region and a finite internal reservoir to maintain concentration near the process. The original demonstration was urea crystallization, not ionic nanopore conduction. | A possible concentration-control architecture, but not evidence that one can delete the water reservoir in the existing NAMD system. The finite reservoir can deplete. |

## What a partially implicit implementation would require

The GCMC/BD example genuinely replaces remote reservoirs and solvent with reduced descriptions, but it also changes the inner-region dynamics. It is not “the same all-atom NAMD pore with a GBIS bath around its ends.” A fully atomistic central region coupled to a coarse-grained, ideal-gas or continuum exterior is another category: adaptive resolution/open-system molecular dynamics. Such methods require controlled exchange, density/chemical-potential compensation and interface treatment. See [Delle Site et al., *From adaptive resolution to molecular dynamics of open systems* (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8547219/). This is a general methods reference, not validation of a ready-made DNA/graphene ion-current model.

For this project, a hybrid atomistic-pore/continuum-reservoir development would need to preserve both species flux and electrochemical potential across the interface, account for electrostatic response, supply calibrated diffusion/mobility, and keep the interface beyond the atomistically important hydration and access regions. Those are implementation requirements inferred from the methods, not features implemented by this change.

## Recommendation for R1

Keep explicit solvent for the first defensible production baseline. Seal the membrane periodically, enlarge the transport-normal reservoir space, and test size convergence. Check plateau concentrations, water density, open-pore conductance, bulk-electrolyte conductivity and current uncertainty with the same force field and thermostat. An implicit external reservoir does not eliminate the physical access resistance that the experiment measures.

Use CompEL if maintaining unequal concentrations is the primary requirement and the added explicit-system cost is acceptable. Consider GCMC/BD or NP+LEMC as a separate reduced-model project for broad parameter sweeps. Validate it against the atomistic baseline, especially where DNA motion, ion binding, hydration or graphene edge chemistry determines selectivity.

## Implemented setup changes

New graphene packages extend a commensurate restrained honeycomb wall over the final Cartesian cell, after automatic or manual box sizing. Tangential lattice repeat counts and small fitting scale factors are recorded in the graphene descriptor. This remains an idealized neutral wall, not a bonded elastic graphene force field. The wall is position-restrained and the cell is fixed-volume, including production descendants: Cartesian restraint references cannot follow arbitrary lateral cell dilation without changing seam coverage.

Fixed-volume solvation needs density validation. The change is not a claim that initial GROMACS water placement is an equilibrated 1-atm bath. A future normal-pressure equilibration path could orient the sheet in XY and use constant-area pressure coupling; NAMD's `useConstantArea` fixes XY while allowing Z fluctuations, so it is not an axis-independent switch for the existing Y-normal R1 geometry. [NAMD pressure-control documentation](https://www-s.ks.uiuc.edu/Research/namd/3.0/ug/node39.html).

The wizard accepts partial X/Y/Z overrides in nm. Null axes retain automatic sizing; reset clears all overrides. Displayed automatic sizes are estimates from the current design, with final measurements taken during preparation. Manual sizes are preserved and validated. Existing prepared jobs require rebuilding to obtain the new wall; completed trajectories are not repaired retroactively. No implicit-reservoir engine was added.
