An **overhang** is a portion of a strand that is embedded in a scaffolded (duplex) structure — it begins (or ends) within the double-stranded region and extends outward beyond it. Overhangs are NOT standalone strands; by definition they originate on a scaffolded helix (helix 0/1 in the bundle) and their free end hangs off into the overhang helix.

**Why:** The user explicitly stated this when asked about strand terminus topology for a 3-helix overhang design. "Overhangs by definition are parts of strands that are embedded in a scaffolded structure."

**How to apply:** When reasoning about where strand termini appear on overhang helices, remember that one end of the overhang strand is always on the main bundle helix (not on the overhang helix). Only the free tip of the overhang creates a terminus on the overhang helix — the crossover-entry end does not (the strand continues onto the main helix there).
